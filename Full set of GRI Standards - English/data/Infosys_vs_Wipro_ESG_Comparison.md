## 3. FRAMEWORKS USED — HOW THEY APPROACH REPORTING

### Infosys: Multi-Framework, Future-Ready Approach

```
GRI 2021 (Core framework for impacts)
     +
SASB (Sector-specific, IT industry metrics)
     +
TCFD (Climate-related financial risk disclosures)
     +
UN Global Compact (Human rights, labor, environment, anti-corruption)
     +
ESRS/CSRD (EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive — double materiality)
     +
UN SDGs (17 goals alignment)
```

**Infosys also uses DOUBLE MATERIALITY** — a more advanced approach that assesses:
- **Impact materiality**: What does Infosys do TO the world?
- **Financial materiality**: What does the world (climate risks, regulations, stakeholder expectations) do TO Infosys?

This makes Infosys's report more aligned with European regulatory requirements (CSRD) and more comprehensive in identifying risks AND responsibilities.

### Wipro: Established, Stakeholder-Centric Approach

```
GRI (Primary — since 2008)
     +
ISO 26000 (Social responsibility guidelines — since FY 2019-20)
     +
AA1000 Principles (Stakeholder engagement quality)
     +
UN SDGs (9 goals aligned)
```

**Wipro uses IMPACT MATERIALITY** — consistent with GRI's own definition. However, Wipro's Chairman explicitly acknowledges the growing complexity of multiple frameworks and warns against "compliance fatigue" and "checkbox-ticking mentality."

**Key Insight**: Infosys is moving toward EU-style double materiality (future regulations), while Wipro is deeply embedded in GRI's original impact-first philosophy. Both are valid; Infosys is more future-proofed for global compliance.

## 4. MATERIALITY ASSESSMENT — GRI 3 COMPARISON

### What GRI 3 requires:
Step 1: Understand context → Step 2: Identify impacts → Step 3: Assess significance → Step 4: Prioritize for reporting

### Infosys Materiality Process (GRI 3-1)

6-step process:
1. **Value Chain Identification** — Map activities, resources, relationships across upstream/downstream value chain
2. **Determine ESG Matters** — Identify ESG topics relevant to IT industry, sub-topics, side topics
3. **Identify Impacts, Risks and Opportunities** — Comprehensive review of all 131 GRI-related impacts; built 181 IROs (Impacts, Risks, Opportunities)
4. **Stakeholder Engagement** — Consultations with investors, clients, employees, suppliers/partners, governments, communities
5. **Determine Impact and Financial Materiality** — Scored on scale, scope, irremediability (impact) AND likelihood of financial effect (financial)
6. **Finalize Material Matters** — Workshops with ESG Council; threshold defined; approved by Board

**Infosys Materiality Matrix Results (GRI 3-2)**:

| Tier | Material Topics |
|---|---|
| Very High (Both Dimensions) | Climate Change, Data Privacy, Information Management, Corporate Governance, Enabling Digital Talent at Scale |
| High | Water, Employee Wellness & Experience, Diversity & Inclusion, Tech for Good, Emerging Local Communities |
| Medium | Waste, Acquisitions & Investments, Nurturing Local Community Inclusivity |

**Special Note**: Infosys merged "AI regulations and frameworks" and "GenAI advancements" into a single theme — "Amplifying Communities" — reflecting how AI is reshaping both their business risk and social impact simultaneously.

---

### Wipro Materiality Process (GRI 3-1)

Wipro's process starts from **stakeholder engagement** (GRI + AA1000):
- Engaged with employees, customers, investors, suppliers, local communities, governments
- Identified 50 topics → distilled into **25 key issues** across **8 themes**
- Used multiple consultation channels (forums, surveys, direct meetings)
- Board's ESG Committee reviews and approves material topics annually

**Wipro's 7 Sustainability Dashboard Goals (Material Topics)**:
1. Climate Change
2. Water Stewardship
3. Diversity, Equity & Inclusion
4. Continuous Learning
5. Health & Well-being
6. Community Impact
7. Responsible Technology Governance

**Wipro Materiality Matrix Results (GRI 3-2)**:
- Environmental Impact (most material for external stakeholders)
- Corporate Social Responsibility
- Health and Safety
- Human Rights
- Business Conduct

**Key Difference**: Infosys uses a formal **double materiality matrix** with two axes (impact significance vs. financial materiality). Wipro's matrix is a **single-axis stakeholder priority** map. Infosys's approach is more rigorous and GRI 3-compliant in terms of separating and quantifying impact severity, scope, and irremediability.

**Wipro's governance chain is more explicit and GRI-aligned** in terms of showing the exact path of responsibility from Board to operational implementation.

## 11. REPORTING PHILOSOPHY — THE BIG PICTURE DIFFERENCE

### Infosys: "From Insight to Impact"
- **Philosophy**: Use data and technology to generate insights → translate them into measurable impact
- Treats sustainability as **core business strategy**, not a separate CSR function
- **AI-first lens**: Every ESG initiative is being reimagined through the lens of AI and data
- Forward-looking: Preparing for EU CSRD/ESRS regulations even as an Indian company
- More ambitious environmental targets (carbon positive by 2030 vs. net zero by 2040)
- Separate ESG Data Book for detailed quantitative disclosure

### Wipro: "Leading with Resilience and Responsibility"
- **Philosophy**: Sustainability as stewardship — responsible management for all stakeholders
- GRI as the "gold standard" since 2008 — deeply embedded in the organization
- Explicitly warns against "compliance fatigue" — advocates substance over form
- First company to publish **Environmental Profit & Loss** (8 years) — monetizes environmental impact
- More nuanced on governance: openly discusses limitations and evolving frameworks
- Strong community focus through Wipro Foundation (ecology, education, healthcare)

## 16. CONCLUSION — THE ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

**Infosys** leads on environmental ambition, materiality rigor, certifications, and future regulatory alignment. Having been carbon neutral since 2020 and targeting climate positivity by 2030, they are ahead of most peers. Their adoption of double materiality and EU CSRD-preparedness shows they are reporting for a global, forward-looking audience.

**Wipro** leads on GRI transparency, reporting history, and stakeholder accountability. Their 17-year GRI journey, explicit GRI code labeling throughout the report, Environmental P&L statement, and candid acknowledgment of the risks of "checkbox compliance" demonstrate a deeply embedded and philosophically mature sustainability culture.

**For a student or analyst**: Wipro is easier to read and verify against GRI standards (every disclosure labeled). Infosys has more sophisticated methodology and better environmental data. Both companies demonstrate strong GRI 1 compliance, robust GRI 2 disclosures, and well-structured GRI 3 material topic management — making them both excellent examples of IT sector sustainability reporting in India.
