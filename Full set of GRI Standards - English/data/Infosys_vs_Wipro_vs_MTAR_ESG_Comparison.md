### Why MTAR Uses BRSR and Not GRI

BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) is India's **mandatory** sustainability framework introduced by SEBI in 2021. It is required for the top 1,000 listed companies by market cap. MTAR files BRSR as required by law.

GRI is **voluntary** — Infosys and Wipro choose to report under GRI because they serve global clients and investors who expect international standards. MTAR's primarily domestic manufacturing focus means BRSR compliance is their benchmark.

## 9. INDUSTRY CONTEXT — WHY SECTOR MATTERS

This is crucial for fair comparison. These three companies face fundamentally different ESG challenges:

### IT Services (Infosys & Wipro) — ESG Profile:
- **Main emissions**: Buildings (energy), employee travel, data centres
- **Main social risk**: Labour practices, diversity, digital divide
- **Governance complexity**: High (global operations, diverse regulations)
- **Environmental improvement levers**: Clean energy is straightforward (switch to RE)
- **Carbon reduction is relatively easier** — no industrial processes

### Aerospace & Defence Manufacturing (MTAR) — ESG Profile:
- **Main emissions**: Manufacturing processes, energy for heavy machinery
- **Main environmental risks**: Hazardous waste (metals, chemicals), water discharge
- **Main social risk**: Worker safety (high-risk manufacturing environment), male-dominated workforce
- **Governance**: Simpler structure; but defence sector = national security sensitivity
- **Carbon reduction is harder** — cannot easily replace industrial heat/processes with solar

### The SASB Ecosystem Risk for MTAR:
SES identifies MTAR's upstream industries (Auto Components, Iron & Steel, Electrical Equipment) as having higher ESG risks, which flow into MTAR's risk profile. If MTAR's suppliers have ESG issues, MTAR is also exposed — this is the GRI concept of "value chain" impact (GRI 2-6).

**Output risk** is also dual:
- "In case of war" — MTAR's products (nuclear equipment, aerospace components) could cause environmental destruction and loss of life
- "As savior" — MTAR's products could also save lives (defense systems, nuclear energy as clean power)

## 12. THE FUNDAMENTAL DIFFERENCE IN REPORTING MATURITY

### Infosys — "Leading Reporter"

## 13. KEY LEARNINGS FROM THIS COMPARISON

### Lesson 1: Industry context determines ESG priorities
IT companies naturally score higher on Environment because switching to renewable energy is simpler. Manufacturing companies face harder environmental challenges. Comparing their raw scores directly is misleading.

### Lesson 2: Self-reporting vs external rating gives different pictures
Infosys and Wipro control their narrative — they highlight strengths and provide context for weaknesses. MTAR's SES report shows an independent analyst calling out both positives (ethics, cybersecurity, audit) AND negatives (fatality, LTIFR, waste growth) equally. External ratings are more candid.

### Lesson 3: Disclosure quality matters as much as performance
MTAR's renewable energy jumped to 98.73% — better than Infosys and Wipro — but still scores low on energy because it doesn't disclose targets, intensity, or comparable data. GRI's principle: **good performance without good disclosure counts for less**.

### Lesson 4: GRI creates a level playing field; BRSR is India-centric
When comparing across borders, GRI allows standardized comparison. BRSR is mandatory in India but not comparable internationally. Companies like MTAR that only use BRSR are invisible to global ESG investors.

### Lesson 5: The Parivartan Score reveals trajectory
A score today tells you WHERE a company is. The Parivartan (transition) score tells you WHERE it's going. MTAR's 31/100 Parivartan score suggests it is not yet systematically improving its ESG performance year-on-year.

### Lesson 6: Small companies can excel in governance
MTAR's 89/100 governance score rivals the IT giants. Smaller, focused companies often have cleaner, more auditable governance structures. Complexity is a governance risk.
